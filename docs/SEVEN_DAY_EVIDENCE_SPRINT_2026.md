# Seven-day evidence sprint — 13–19 October 2026

Run exploratory **nonclinical** research only. Do not collect health details, names, diagnoses, trauma histories, suicidal thoughts or medical records. Research participants should be consenting adults. Findings should be anonymized; no private notes belong in a public GitHub repository.

## Youth usability questions (5–8 minutes)
1. If a friend needed mental-health support in Zimbabwe, where would they first look?
2. What information would they check before reaching out (fees, legitimacy, privacy, language, location)?
3. What could make the first contact difficult without telling us anyone's private history?
4. Would they want to skip AI and go straight to a verified human? Why?
5. Would a neutral editable first-contact draft be useful or uncomfortable?
6. Test the live prototype: which step is unclear or confusing on this phone?
7. What would make them trust or distrust this service?

**Report honestly**, e.g. “5 interviews conducted on 16 October; 3 found provider fee information difficult to locate” only if those details are true. Do not claim statistical representativeness.

## Message to ask for clinical feedback

We have a working nonclinical prototype, but no live AI therapy or referral service. We are not claiming an existing partnership or clinical endorsement. Your advice could help us design more responsibly.

Would you be available for a brief conversation before 20 October?

Thank you.

# Seed grant implementation plan — US$5,000 maximum

This is a **proposed 12-month pilot budget**, not an approved award, provider quotation or guarantee of outcomes. Scope is constrained to a consent-first pathway from youth expression to verified support, not a commercial nationwide AI therapy product.

## Milestone-based roadmap

| Time | Activities | Deliverable | Release/safety gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1–2 | 20–30 voluntary listening interviews with young adults; 4–6 professional/provider interviews; map existing services | De-identified needs findings, accessibility requirements, service-map | No personal clinical histories retained; consent and research governance |
| M3–4 | Clinical and safeguarding co-design; privacy, security, legality and referral governance review | Approved protocol, initial partner letters, data-flow diagrams | No live care/referral or AI processing before signed oversight |
| M5–6 | Provider credential checking, user testing, accessible low-data UX, build consent-first secure pilot workflow | Pilot-ready product and verified partner inventory | Provider agreement and independent verification documented |
| M7–8 | Controlled voluntary pilot with ~50 consenting adults aged 18–35, subject to approvals | Log of connection requests and verified outcomes (minimal data) | Named incident response owners and incident process |
| M9–10 | Analyze barriers, access, affordability and user feedback; independently examine harms | Evaluation report and recommendations | No unsupported clinical-efficacy claims |
| M11–12 | Improve pilot, validate finance model, decide scale/pause/stop | Sustainability plan, grant report, next-district roadmap | Clinical, privacy and user-acceptance go/no-go decision |

| Use | USD | Justification |

| Privacy, security, and legal/compliance review | 800 | Independent assessment before any sensitive-data pilot |
| Accessible, low-data software pilot engineering | 900 | Consent-first care navigation, device/browser testing |
| Partner outreach and credential verification | 750 | Provider discovery, onsite verification and agreements |
| Participant accessibility and research support | 1,000 | Data/transport/access accommodations, consented participation expenses |
| Monitoring, evaluation and learning | 500 | Predefined metrics, de-identified reports, usability assessments |
| Contingency | 50 | Minor unforeseen eligible costs |
| **Total** | **5,000** | Milestone payments and reporting required under award terms |

**Not budgeted:** unapproved clinical treatment, medical prescriptions, AI risk-scoring, 24/7 counseling staff, nationwide operations, or a production-grade medical device. If mandated safeguarding requires more cost, shrink or pause the pilot rather than weaken protection.

## Measurable pilot outcomes — targets, never achievements

- Recruit up to 50 eligible consenting adults after approvals; 20–30 discovery interviewees separately (not necessarily pilot participants).
- Obtain at least **two participating verified service partners** before activating real introduction requests.
- Define and report denominator-based metrics: number starting service navigation, number identifying an appropriate verified option, number consenting to referral, number with documented first human contact, and follow-through times.
- Test navigation comprehension and mobile usability with people who use entry-level Android devices or lower-data access.
- Publish aggregated safety and inclusion findings, including drop-off and unsuccessful navigation rather than cherry-picked success.
- **No quantified clinical benefit target** is claimed without a proper study design; do not infer mental-health improvement from chat sessions.

## Financial sustainability hypothesis

The user-facing care navigator should remain free. Explore paid service-navigation support from universities, employers and health programmes **only after** validated benefit, appropriate conflict-of-interest management and clinical/ethical review. Never monetize personal conversations, sell sensitive data, require advertising to access help or steer users toward the highest-paying provider. The pilot tests demand and delivery costs rather than claiming revenue already exists.

## Simple theory of change

If young people face stigma, uncertainty and high friction accessing services, then private, voluntary self-expression **plus** clear verified care choices **plus** human-consent-first introductions should make the initial help-seeking step easier. If that pathway works, more participants should reach credible support. Evidence must include actual contacts and user feedback, not just visits or AI engagement.

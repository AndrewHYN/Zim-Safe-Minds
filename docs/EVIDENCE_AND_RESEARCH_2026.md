# Evidence, differentiation and field research plan

## What is known from public information

1. The official HackLab Category 1 validated **social and cultural stigma**, youth-unfriendly health services, information/digital gaps and health-financing barriers as root causes: https://zimbabwe.unfpa.org/en/submission/zimbabwe-youth-innovation-hacklab-2entity26
2. UNICEF's 2023 country report describes stigma and limited reliable data on children/adolescent mental health in Zimbabwe. Do **not** treat these data as direct estimates of the 18–35 pilot population: https://www.unicef.org/zimbabwe/reports/case-mental-health-children-and-adolescents-zimbabwe
3. Friendship Bench already offers free online support by trained supporters via WhatsApp and runs Youth Friendship Bench. It is an **existing service, not a Zim Safe Minds partner**: https://www.friendshipbenchzimbabwe.org/online and https://www.friendshipbenchzimbabwe.org/youth-friendship-bench
4. In 2026, Zimbabwean Youth Friendship Bench researchers described co-designing digitalized lived-experience tools: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44202-026-00713-8. This shows digital mental-health innovation is not new in Zimbabwe; our proposed distinction must be tested.

## Competitive landscape, stated carefully

**Existing alternatives:** Friendship Bench online/peer support; other clinics and providers offering counseling; informal referrals by friends and family. These serve real people and may already have well-designed handoff processes. We cannot claim nobody has done chat or peer support before.

**Hypothesis to validate:** Users may benefit from a standalone privacy-first navigator that doesn't pretend to provide care, helps clarify non-sensitive preferences, compares verified options by cost/availability/language and allows user-controlled introductions across providers. Its independent value depends on interviews with young people and providers.

**Go/no-go question:** If people can already find, trust and reach a suitable service without such a layer, then Zim Safe Minds should pivot or stop instead of duplicating existing programs.

## Questions for 20–30 youth interviews (voluntary, nonclinical)

- Without naming personal conditions: where would you start if you wanted mental-health support?
- What would make contacting a provider awkward or difficult?
- How would you check whether a service is genuine, affordable and respectful?
- Would you use a quick preference chooser before seeing human help options? Why?
- Would an opt-in introduction help, or would you prefer contacting services yourself?
- How do connectivity cost, phone access, disability support and languages change your choice?
- Would you trust a chatbot to organize your thoughts? What would make it feel unsafe?
- What information would you **not** want an app to collect or retain?

Record only voluntary, minimal non-identifying findings in approved research notes. Do not solicit diagnoses, suicidal thoughts, names or therapy details in a public online form.

## Questions for 4–6 provider/clinical consultations

- What services and referral routes already exist for 18–35-year-olds?
- What credential and facility verification procedures are appropriate?
- How should availability, fees, accessibility and online modality be represented accurately?
- What clinical exclusions/urgent pathways and consent model would be required?
- What information can safely accompany an opt-in introduction, if any?
- Who responds when a user cannot reach care, and during what hours?
- How could this support existing programs instead of competing with them?

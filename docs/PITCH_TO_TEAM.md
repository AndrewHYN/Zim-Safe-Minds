# Pitch Zim Safe Minds to the Team

This is an **internal concept pitch** for prospective teammates to critique and improve, not a claim that we've already won funding or established clinical partnerships.

## One-sentence idea

**Zim Safe Minds makes the first step toward mental-health help easier for young Zimbabweans: optional AI-guided reflection, practical wellbeing tools and a privacy-first route to real human care.**

## 60-second pitch — read aloud

> Think of a young person in Zimbabwe who is struggling but doesn't want to tell family, friends or a professional yet. They might be worried about stigma, how much help will cost, or simply not know whom to contact.
>
> Zim Safe Minds is our answer to that first, difficult step. We want people to have a comfortable place to put feelings into words, access small grounding tools, and find trustworthy human support without pressure. Our long-term vision includes AI-assisted reflection, but **the prototype we've built today is a clearly labeled scripted demo**, not an AI therapist.
>
> What makes our idea worth testing is the bridge to action. Users choose preferences like price, language and online or in-person care. They can prepare their own first message without sharing private chat history. The future pilot could connect users to verified professionals with consent.
>
> We already have a working website. With the US$5,000 HackLab seed grant, we would validate the need, work with qualified clinical advisers, test accessible care navigation and measure how many people really reach support.
>
> Our success metric isn't how long someone chats with a bot. It's whether a young person can take the first step toward real care. **Zim Safe Minds: a safe space to talk, a path to real support.**

## Ask the team these questions

- What real problem would make someone use this instead of just searching online or contacting an existing clinic?
- What is the *most useful* part today: reflection, grounding, care navigation or help with the first message?
- How can we validate the claim that stigma and uncertainty are barriers, without collecting people's private mental-health histories?
- What changes would improve trust on a low-cost Android phone?
- Which roles do we each *want* to own, and who wants to coordinate the application and pitch?

## What the US$5,000 is for (no need to debate every line item today)

A focused, 12-month pilot: youth research, qualified clinical/safeguarding input, privacy and accessibility checks, independently verifying service partners, and evaluating whether consenting users can reach actual support. The detailed costed budget is maintained separately by the operations owner: [budget & roadmap](MILESTONES_BUDGET_2026.md).

## Three honest answers everyone should know

**Is there a live AI therapist?** No. AI-assisted reflective conversation is a key *planned* feature. Today the interactive replies are scripted. We aren't offering therapy or crisis monitoring.

**Do we have therapists signed up?** Not yet. We link to an independent existing service, but have no verified partner agreements. Real introductions must be consent-based and clinically reviewed.

**Why won't we just duplicate Friendship Bench?** Friendship Bench already provides important support. Our hypothesis is that a separate, accessible first-contact navigator reduces friction across the care-seeking journey. We should **test** this and adapt if youth and professionals say the gap isn't real.

## What a successful kickoff looks like

Everyone can explain Zim Safe Minds in plain language, has tested the demo, has chosen a meaningful role voluntarily, agrees who will be the Team Lead and knows the next action before the 20 October deadline. No fabricated evidence, no forced clinical feature launch.
